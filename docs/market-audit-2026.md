# ByO Leads on Breadth, Loses on Phones

No app on sale in November 2026, in Australia or anywhere else, does what Build Your Own (ByO) does from start to finish: take a homeowner's plain-English brief and return a parametric 3D house with an itemised Australian construction cost, a build programme, NCC references and a quote. Each of those pieces exists somewhere else. Maket 2.0 and Homestyler turn text into plans and renders but give no cost. Houzz Pro, Handoff and Higharc turn descriptions or plans into estimates for US builders. BuildLedger produces Australian phase-by-phase estimates from text, but for builders and without 3D. Buildxact leads small-builder estimating and job management. Archistar gives developers free generative massing. hipages, Airtasker and iBuildNew control the homeowner-to-builder funnel. ByO's Site Office goes further than any residential tool found on retention-aware progress claims, critical path on an Australian working-day calendar, diary-to-EOT evidence and an approvals checklist, and it costs **A$149 a month against Buildxact Pro's A$339–399** ([SoftwareHQ](https://softwarehq.pages.dev/review/buildxact)). ByO loses on the things the market leaders are rated for: phone apps that never lose work, keep working offline, send a quote in two taps and pass app review. Today's desktop interface squeezed onto a phone is worse than even Buildxact's poorly rated Onsite companion app, and a repackaged website is exactly what Apple's rule 4.2 rejects ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Three other risks matter: credibility (no estimator on the market has an independent accuracy audit, and AI-drawn plans are widely criticised); an empty marketplace up against hipages' **1.26 million Australian jobs a year**; and a 1.5% contract fee that has no Australian precedent and no point at which it can be enforced. The prescription is to keep the deterministic cost engine and the Australian contract-admin logic, fix phone reliability and cost transparency before submitting to the app stores, leave the studio and takeoff tools on the computer, and build next what builders treat as table stakes (online quote acceptance, an offline site diary and a homeowner portal), which turn ByO's unique data into a loop that ends in a signed contract.

## No competitor turns a plain-English brief into a costed Australian house

Each ByO capability is done well by somebody. The finding that matters is that **nobody connects them**, and that the companies with the most money are all building for builders, not homeowners.

### Consumer design apps draw houses but never price them

The "design your own house" category is made up of manual drag-and-drop floor-plan and furnishing tools. **Planner 5D** leads on scale with **50M+ Google Play installs and 600K reviews at 4.3 stars** ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.planner5d.planner5d&hl=en_AU&gl=AU)), and **Homestyler** claims 200M+ users and a catalogue of 1M+ branded models ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.autodesk.homestyler&hl=en_AU&gl=AU)); the rest of the field is Dassault's HomeByMe, Room Planner, Home Design 3D and Live Home 3D. Most add AR furniture preview and LiDAR room scanning (iPhone and iPad Pro only), and since 2025 they have added AI copilots.

Plain-English input is starting to appear. HomeByMe's AI Planner Assistant edits designs by text command but needs iOS 27 and an Apple Intelligence device ([App Store AU](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/homebyme-house-planner-3d/id1465715066)). Homestyler's AI Planner charges 15 credits per plan ([Homestyler pricing](https://www.homestyler.com/pricing)). **Maket 2.0** (launched 2 October 2026, web only) builds a plan through a chat that asks about floors, outline and room counts, then renders rooms in 3D and exports PDF/DXF ([TestingCatalog](https://www.testingcatalog.com/maket-2-0-brings-ai-floor-plans-and-3d-home-renders/)).

**None of them returns an itemised construction cost, a timeline or a quote for a whole house.** The closest equivalents are desktop-only, priced for professionals, or limited to furniture. Chief Architect's Home Designer keeps a materials list on Mac/PC for US$495 a year ([Home Designer](https://www.homedesignersoftware.com/)). magicplan's estimator calculates quantities from sketches but makes the user supply unit prices, costs **A$129.99 a month** on the AU App Store, and ships ready-made price lists for US users only ([App Store AU](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/magicplan/id427424432); [magicplan help](https://help.magicplan.app/estimate-materials-and-costs)). Planner 5D lists a "Price Estimator", but whether it prices construction or just furniture is unconfirmed ([Planner 5D pricing](https://planner5d.com/pricing)).

magicplan's own developer told a reviewer that the app makes "floor plans, not building plans" ([App Store AU](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/magicplan/id427424432)). That line sums up the whole category.

### Australian cost tools stop at a $/m² figure or a sales funnel

Australian homeowners see four kinds of cost tool, nearly all free and nearly all paid for by someone else. Lead-generation sites publish static $/m² guides: Airtasker's former Oneflare guide was last updated in June 2024 and still quotes $900–$3,100/m² ([Airtasker](https://www.airtasker.com/au/costs/building-construction/house-building-cost/?of-redirect=true&domain=oneflare.com.au)). Quantity-surveying (QS) firms run form calculators as lead magnets: Duo Tax asks about 13 questions, then wants name, mobile and email before it will export one figure with a low/high band ([Duo Tax](https://duotax.com.au/construction-cost-calculator/)). Volume-builder configurators price only the builder's own designs: Simonds' Emerge offers about 30 Victorian designs "from $226,100", with no site costs and no 3D ([Simonds](https://simonds.com.au/vic/house-designs/filtered/design-your-own-home-online-victoria)). Insurers offer rebuild calculators such as Cordell Sum Sure ([Cotality](https://www.cotality.com/products/cordellsumsure)).

The newest entrants are AI estimators. **BuildLedger** is the closest functional analogue to ByO's estimator: it takes a typed or spoken description and returns a phase-by-phase estimate with materials, labour, plant, markup, contingency and GST that converts to a quote, but it is web-only, sold to builders, and has no 3D or programme ([BuildLedger](https://buildledger.com.au/free-estimate)). **SimplyWise** is the strongest global app aimed at homeowners as well as contractors; it turns a photo plus a description into a line-item PDF quote and has **4.74 stars from about 17K Android ratings**, but it prices in US terms ([AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.simplywise.costestimator)).

No homeowner tool found anywhere produces a construction programme or NCC references at clause level.

### Builder software splits into takeoff suites, trade apps and US AI startups

**Builder estimating.** Australian builder estimating is led by **Buildxact**: founded in Australia in 2011, it raised an A$18.5M Series A in 2022 at a valuation above A$125M ([Buildxact](https://www.buildxact.com/company/about/); [Startup Daily](https://www.startupdaily.net/2022/02/construction-tech-startup-buildxact-nails-18-5-million-series-a/)). The legacy incumbents are Cubit, from Buildsoft (40+ years in estimating), and Databuild, a Windows-only desktop application ([Buildsoft](https://www.buildsoft.com.au/); [Databuild FAQ](https://www.databuild.com.au/faq/)). Groundplan sells plan takeoff to 8,000+ contractors and plugs into trade job apps ([Groundplan](https://groundplan.com/llms.txt)).

**Trade apps.** Trades quote from price books on their phones using Tradify, Fergus (26,000+ tradies), ServiceM8 and Simpro Group's simPRO and AroFlo ([Fergus](https://fergus.com/supplier-partners); [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/simpro-group-acquires-delight-to-add-ai-customer-engagement-and-drive-repeat-revenue)).

**AI estimating** arrives either as paid add-ons to incumbents, such as Buildxact's Blu (July 2025) and Cubit AI Estimating ([Buildxact news](https://news.buildxact.com/96196-introducing-blu-your-new-ai-powered-construction-assistant); [Truespeak](https://truespeak.io/blog/construction-estimating-software-australia-2026)), or from well-funded US startups such as Handoff, Beam AI (US$30.5M Series B) and Bobyard (US$35M Series A) ([Handoff](https://handoff.ai/blog/handoff-raises-5-8m-for-residential-construction); [Insight Partners](https://www.insightpartners.com/ideas/attentive-ai-secures-30-5-million-series-b-to-accelerate-ai-innovation-in-construction/); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251210946193/en/Bobyard-Raises-$35-Million-Series-A-to-Power-How-the-World-Gets-Built)). Local AI startups are moving quickly: Melbourne's **EstiMate** claimed US$100K ARR six weeks after its public launch ([ARR Club](https://www.arr.club/signal/estimate-hit-100k-arr-six-weeks-after-public-launch)).

**Job management.** In Australia, residential job management is effectively Buildxact for small builders and Databuild or ClickHome for established and volume builders ([Capterra AU](https://www.capterra.com.au/compare/124483/173135/databuild-estimating-software/vs/buildxact)). US platforms (Buildertrend, JobTread, Projul) are built around QuickBooks; JobTread is the fastest-growing, with **10,000 customers by February 2026** ([JobTread](https://www.jobtread.com/news/jobtread-surpasses-10000-customers-highlighting-rapid-shift-in-construction-industry)). Progress claims that handle retention and the Security of Payment Act (SOPA) live in commercial tools such as Procore and Autodesk-owned Payapps ([Procore AU](https://en-au.support.procore.com/products/online/user-guide/project-level/invoicing/release-notes?mt-view=f1); [Payapps](https://www.payapps.com/pricing)).

### Marketplaces and AI capital both bypass the homeowner's new build

**Marketplaces.** hipages leads, with **FY26 revenue of $90.6m, about 1.26 million Australian jobs and 33.0k paying subscription businesses** ([hipages FY26](https://bulletin.webull.com/qbd/announcement/20260824/499502010/34d7cda61638004a4ec56dcd19deb731.pdf)). Airtasker booked 898k tasks at a 21.7% take rate ([StockWireX](https://stockwirex.com/?p=40522)). Oneflare closed on 30 June 2026 ([Oneflare](https://help.oneflare.com.au/hc/en-au/articles/58074479988377-Oneflare-is-closing-here-s-what-you-need-to-know)).

**Where the money goes.** Capital in AI-native design is concentrated in builder platforms. Higharc raised a **US$95M Series C in June 2026** (US$170M+ in total) for a plan-image → 3D → takeoff → estimate pipeline sold to US production builders ([Higharc](https://higharc.com/newsroom/higharc-95m-series-c-for-homebuilding-ai)). Maket, the homeowner-facing generator, is seed-funded ([TestingCatalog](https://www.testingcatalog.com/maket-2-0-brings-ai-floor-plans-and-3d-home-renders/)). Archistar's free 3D generative designer names developers, builders and architects as its users, not homeowners ([Archistar pricing](https://www.archistar.ai/pricing/)).

ByO's catalogues compete with recommended retail prices, not with negotiated trade pricing.

### What the planned phone app gets right

Most of the plan matches what the leaders do. **Bottom tabs** follow Apple and Google, which both prescribe three to five labelled destinations ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars.json); [Android](https://developer.android.google.cn/develop/ui/compose/components/navigation-bar?hl=ca)). **Steppers and chips instead of sliders** follow configurator research, which says to start from smart preselected defaults so that "no step should require some sort of active selection" ([Smashing Magazine](https://smashingmagazine.com/2018/02/designing-a-perfect-responsive-configurator)), and they avoid the finger-drawing problem behind Planner 5D's complaints about tiny wall labels and rooms too small to select. **A live price beside the 3D** follows the configurator rule to "display the price next to the product's rendering" and keep it live ([Smashing Magazine](https://smashingmagazine.com/2018/02/designing-a-perfect-responsive-configurator)); Tesla's lesson applies too, showing the real total first and largest with caveats secondary ([InsideEVs](https://insideevs.com/news/651527/tesla-us-site-shows-actual-purchase-price-up-front/)). **A real PDF through the share sheet** matches the Joist and Tradify build-preview-send loop ([Joist](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/contractor-estimating-and-invoicing-tool-by-joist/id592163563)). **A builder Jobs tab with site diary photos and variations** targets the exact gap Buildxact Onsite users complain about: a 2023 reviewer said it could not handle on-site variations that the PC version can ([App Store AU](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/buildxact-onsite/id1297866447)).

**Keeping high-rise, multi-dwelling, takeoff, imports, IFC/DXF, film and 3D Studio on the computer** has clear precedent. Houzz Pro blocks new takeoffs and 3D plan edits on mobile and shows an empty state that prompts users "to create one on your desktop computer" ([Houzz Pro](https://pro.houzz.com/pro-help/r/how-to-use-takeoffs-on-houzz-pro-app)), and RoomSketcher says plainly that "drawing isn't available on phones" ([RoomSketcher](https://www.roomsketcher.com/features/mobile/)).

### What the plan still misses

**Navigation.** Five tabs is the ceiling, and none of the leaders gives settings a tab: Houzz Pro, Procore and Buildxact Onsite put account and settings behind a profile avatar or gear ([Procore](https://support.procore.com/product-releases/new-releases/mobile-new-sidebar-and-home-screen-experience-for-procore-ios-and-android-apps); [Buildxact Onsite help](https://help.buildxact.com/en/articles/8388056-what-is-buildxact-onsite)). Moving Account to an avatar leaves homeowners three tabs and builders four. Apple's guidance also says that if a section is empty, the app should "explain why" rather than hide it ([Apple HIG](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars.json)), so a Market tab with few or no listings is a credibility problem on day one.

**The quote loop stops at a PDF.** Tradify Pro, ServiceM8 proposals and Joist all close the loop with online acceptance or an on-site signature ([Tradify](https://www.tradifyhq.com/au/pricing); [ServiceM8](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/servicem8/id378062736)).

**Reliability.** The site diary must work offline with a queue that syncs later, as Buildertrend's daily logs do ([Buildertrend](https://helpcenter.buildertrend.net/en/articles/10065002-mobile-app-faqs)); Tradify is criticised for lacking this ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/152413/Tradify/reviews/)). The app must autosave and restore state when Android kills a backgrounded WebView, the exact failure behind a Joist review in which switching apps discarded an estimate edit ([Joist](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/contractor-estimating-and-invoicing-tool-by-joist/id592163563)). Guest sessions should be anonymous Supabase accounts that later convert to full accounts without losing data ([vp0](https://vp0.com/blogs/supabase-anonymous-login-ios-ui-wrapper)), because device-only storage loses everything on reinstall, a complaint HomeByMe already carries ([App Store AU](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/homebyme-house-planner-3d/id1465715066)). Sign-in must stay inside the app; ServiceM8 reviewers complain of log-outs "that move you to Safari" ([ServiceM8](https://apps.apple.com/au/app/servicem8/id378062736)). Android's back gesture must route through in-app history, since predictive back is on by default for opted-in apps from Android 15 ([Android Developers](https://developer.android.com/guide/navigation/custom-back/predictive-back-gesture)). And 3D should open as a static hero image that loads an interactive, low-poly, progressively loaded model on tap ([Needle](https://engine.needle.tools/docs/gltf-progressive/)), because Planner 5D's worst reviews are crashes and black screens on the 2D-to-3D switch ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/planner-5d-home-design/id606173978)).

**Rule 4.2.** Apple requires apps to offer "features, content, and UI that elevate it beyond a repackaged website" ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Developers who sell wrapper services report that reviewers test wrapped apps in airplane mode, and that the usual fix is native camera, share, push, sign-in and graceful offline behaviour ([MobiLoud](https://www.mobiloud.com/blog/app-store-review-guidelines-webview-wrapper)). Every reliability fix above also counts toward 4.2 compliance.

Two device inputs belong at the phone start for the same reason: a voice "describe your house" option, as in hipages' video-to-job, Houzz Pro's voice estimates and BuildLedger's voice input, and a photo of a plan fed into the AI plan reader, which is more relevant to new builds than LiDAR (iPhone-Pro-only and room-scale).

**Rule 3.1.1.** Apple requires in-app purchase for unlocking features or subscriptions inside the app, and outside the US it generally restricts steering users to other payment methods ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). ByO has to decide its iOS billing route before submission.

## Five differentiators hold up; seven risks threaten them

### What ByO genuinely has that others do not

**1. A costed house from a brief, for homeowners.** The first differentiator is the homeowner brief-to-costed-house chain itself: member-level 3D, services, itemised Australian cost, programme, NCC references and a quote, with no sign-up gate. The research found no funded product making that combination, and noted that "no tool found produces a construction timeline from a generated house design for homeowners" ([Higharc](https://higharc.com/newsroom/higharc-95m-series-c-for-homebuilding-ai); [Maket](https://www.maket.ai/pricing)).

**2. Australian contract administration in Site Office.** None of the residential competitors reviewed advertises these: retention on progress claims, critical path on a calendar with state public holidays and the Christmas shutdown, site-diary delays flowing into the programme as EOT evidence, and an engineer/certifier/council approvals checklist.

**3. One data model from homeowner to builder.** A homeowner's design can become a builder's quote and then a Site Office job. Houzz Pro's 3D-plan-to-estimate path is the nearest equivalent, and it is US-focused and pro-only ([KBB](https://kbbonline.com/?p=161357)).

**4. Price.** The full builder stack costs about a third of Buildxact Pro, with AI included rather than sold as A$99–149 add-ons.

**5. An ungated free result.** Duo Tax, builder configurators and lead sites all ask for contact details first. Practitioner guidance calls an up-front sign-up screen "the most expensive screen" in a mobile funnel ([Glance](https://thisisglance.com/learning-centre/should-my-app-require-sign-up-before-users-can-explore-features)).

The AI crew is novel: no consumer product with multi-specialist AI advisers was found. But its value is unproven and its liability exposure is real, so treat it as a feature to prove, not a moat.

### Weaknesses and risks

**Accuracy and credibility of the numbers.** **No independent accuracy audit of any consumer estimator, Australian or US, was found**, and every competitor hedges: Duo Tax says "indicative purposes only", BuildLedger "ballpark", Metricon "starting point" ([Duo Tax](https://duotax.com.au/construction-cost-calculator/); [BuildLedger](https://buildledger.com.au/free-estimate); [Metricon](https://www.metricon.com.au/costs-and-finance)). AI estimators already draw specific criticism; a Capterra reviewer said Handoff's estimates "do not reflect real-world costs" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10026318/Handoff/reviews/)). Costs also move fast and unevenly. ABS house-construction output prices rose **5.7% nationally in the year to September 2026, but 10.7% in Tasmania against 3.2% in Victoria** ([ABS](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/economy/price-indexes-and-inflation/producer-price-indexes-australia/sep-2026)), so a rate table that is not refreshed quarterly by state drifts measurably within a year. The 2026 BMT table gives sensible bands to check outputs against: **A$2,176–2,895/m² ex GST** for a Sydney single-level project home, **A$3,047–4,284/m²** for unique designs and **A$4,536–8,123/m²** for architect-designed homes, with other cities at roughly 90–120% of Sydney ([Feasly/BMT](https://www.feasly.com.au/guides/construction-cost-per-square-metre-australia)); figures below about A$1,800/m² for a full new build in 2026 look stale. Geometry is the other credibility risk. RoomSketcher, a competitor, reported that floor plans generated by Claude (the model ByO's AI runs on) were "rarely usable", with overlapping furniture and missing doors ([RoomSketcher](https://www.roomsketcher.com/?p=24280)), and Maket itself says AI handles "70-75%" of schematic work ([Maket blog](https://www.maket.ai/blog/ai-floor-plan-generators-the-future-of-home-design-or-just-hype)). ByO's parametric, rule-based engine is the right answer, but it has to be shown to users, not assumed.

**Compliance language.** Labelling NCC references and "structural" or "compliance" advisers invites users to read the output as certification. Even Archistar's council pre-check tools leave the decision with people: Hawkesbury Council says development application (DA) assessment "remains the business of Council Planning Officers" ([Hawkesbury Council](https://www.hawkesbury.nsw.gov.au/plan-and-build/planning-advice/archistar)).

**App-store rejection and the reliability gap.** The current wrapper is the "repackaged website" rule 4.2 describes. Android ratings run well below iOS across the design category (Live Home 3D 3.2 on Play vs 4.57 on US iOS; Polycam 3.6 vs 4.65) ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.belightsoft.livehome3d&hl=en_AU&gl=AU); [App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/live-home-3d-house-design/id1066784070)). That suggests a weak Android WebView build is punished quickly.

**An empty marketplace.** Listings depend on supply that ByO has not recruited. The obvious value for Australian buyers is plans that are actually buildable here. US plans selling at US$1,000–3,000 a set must be redesigned for the NCC's 8 climate zones and 7-star NatHERS-equivalent energy rules, or BASIX in NSW ([Dream Home Source](https://www.dreamhomesource.com/blog/modifying-your-dream-house-plans); [YourHome](https://www.yourhome.gov.au/buy-build-renovate/building-rating-tools); [NSW Planning Portal](https://www.planningportal.nsw.gov.au/climate-zones)). The prefabAUS directory lists 73 prefab/modular builder-manufacturers, which is a finite and recruitable supply pool ([prefabAUS](https://prefabaus.squarespace.com/a-member-directory)).

**The 1.5% fee.** No Australian platform found charges a percentage on a signed residential build contract. Contracts are signed off-platform, so the fee leaks unless ByO collects the contract. Builderscrack's job fee is the nearest precedent, and how it polices that fee is not public. Lock-in terms are a known regulatory trap: hipages gave the ACCC an undertaking in May 2023 over auto-renewal and cancellation practices ([20 Minute Marketing](https://www.20minutemarketing.com.au/blog/hipages-cost-pricing-explained-and-how-to-rely-on-it-less-2026)). What builders currently spend to win a new-home contract was not found, so whether 1.5% is cheap or expensive against their acquisition cost is unknown.

**Competitive response.** Four players could close ByO's gap: Maket (claiming 1M+ registrations) adding costs, Houzz Pro bringing its AI estimates to Australia, Archistar moving its free generative designer down-market to homeowners, and BuildLedger adding a homeowner mode. Behind them, Buildxact Blu is maturing and US takeoff companies with US$30–95M rounds could localise.

**Breadth spreads ByO thin.** One product is attempting what Planner 5D, Buildxact, Buildertrend and hipages each do as their entire business. Every module is judged against a specialist.

**Sequencing.** Ship items 4 to 14 together: they are the difference between a rejected wrapper and an app that survives its first fifty reviews, and each is cheap compared with the ratings damage of shipping without it. Add items 15 to 18 next as one piece of work, the contract loop, in which a homeowner accepts a quote, the builder runs the job in Site Office, and the homeowner signs variations and sees claims on their phone. Seed the marketplace (item 21) only once there is a reason to sign through ByO.

## Conclusion

The research changes where ByO's moat sits. It is not AI and it is not 3D: text-to-plan and plan-image reading are commoditising quickly, and the best-funded companies (Higharc, Beam, Bobyard) are pouring money into exactly those layers for builders. What no one has is the single Australian data model that runs from a homeowner's sentence to a builder's retention-adjusted progress claim, with the programme, the variations and the EOT evidence attached. Everything that strengthens that thread compounds; everything that competes on rendering, furniture catalogues or listing volume plays to rivals' strengths.

The second shift concerns the phone. The phone app should be the reliable front door to that thread, not a shrunken desktop. Users in this category forgive missing features and do not forgive lost work, crashes, billing tricks or navigation that moves. Buildxact's 3.4-star Onsite app shows that even the local leader has not solved builder phone use, so the opening is real. But ByO can only take it by competing on reliability first and features second. Credibility follows the same logic. The market leaders hedge their numbers as "indicative". ByO's best long-term answer is to show its working: sources, refresh dates and a validation trail. Then its figures become the ones builders and homeowners quote back to each other.
