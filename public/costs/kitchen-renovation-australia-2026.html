What Drives Kitchen Renovation Costs?

Cabinetry is the biggest variable. Flat-pack from Bunnings starts at $2,000; semi-custom polyurethane runs $6,000–$12,000; full custom solid timber or stone-look reaches $15,000–$25,000.

Benchtop material matters: laminate ($800–$1,500), engineered stone ($2,500–$5,000), natural stone ($4,000–$10,000). The edge profile, cutouts for sinks and cooktops, and join locations all add cost.

Layout changes are expensive. Moving the sink means moving plumbing; adding a island means new electrical and possibly structural work for a downlight bulkhead. A like-for-like replacement is the cheapest option.

Appliances swing wildly. A budget Westinghouse oven is $600; a premium Miele or Wolf is $5,000+. Induction cooktops now require a dedicated 32A circuit if upgrading from gas.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a kitchen renovation cost in Sydney vs Melbourne?

Sydney and Melbourne costs are very similar — within 5% of each other. Regional areas (Geelong, Newcastle, Gold Coast) tend to be 10–15% cheaper due to lower labour rates, but material costs are the same or slightly higher due to freight.

How long does a kitchen renovation take?

A cosmetic refresh (new doors, benchtop, splashback) takes 2–3 weeks. A full gut renovation with new plumbing and electrical takes 6–10 weeks. Custom cabinetry has a 4–6 week lead time on top of that.

Do I need council approval for a kitchen renovation?

In most Australian councils, a like-for-like kitchen renovation does not need a building permit. If you're changing the structural layout (removing walls, adding windows) or significantly altering plumbing/gas, you'll likely need a building permit. Always check with your local council.
