**Rules we're taking from it**
1. **The 3D result goes in the first 2 seconds.** Show the payoff first and explain after.
2. **Keep it short and make it loop.** Reels under 15 seconds that loop (plan → house → plan) get rewatched.
3. **A face or a real space helps.** Screen-recording-only posts did best when they started with a human reaction or a real unfinished room.
4. **The number is the twist.** Nobody else in the outliers shows *a price*. That's our edge. End on the dollar figure.

## Cinematic build video: code or AI video generator?

**Both are possible. They do different jobs.**

| | In code (three.js, the engine we already have) | AI video generator (Kling, which made the landing-page drone flight) |
|---|---|---|
| Shows *the user's own* house | **Yes.** Exact dimensions, rooms, roof and price | No. It invents a house that only looks similar |
| Cost and speed per video | Free, renders in the browser in about 30–60s | Paid per clip, and takes minutes |
| Look | Clean, stylised 3D (good, not photoreal) | Photoreal: crew, machinery, people |
| Matches the estimate | Stage by stage, with the price climbing | No link to the numbers |

**What it takes in code:** the engine already animates the build (`Engine3D.setProgress(0→1)` reveals foundation → frame → roof → walls → finishes, and ScrollBuild drives it). To make a video, add a scripted camera path, record the canvas with `canvas.captureStream()` + `MediaRecorder`, and save a 9:16 MP4/WebM with the price overlay. Every user gets their own house as an Instagram-ready clip.

**Best of both (premium tier):** render the code version, then send its final frame (or the whole clip) to an AI video model as the reference to get a photoreal "hero" shot of *their* design.
